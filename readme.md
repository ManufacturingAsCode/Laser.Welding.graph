# Laser.Welding.graph

**Conclusion: laser welding is really good for thin sheet metal, and low strength applications!** It is suboptimal for deep root high strength structural welding - in which case stick SMAW is more appropriate.

# Advantages:
Laser welding is a very precisely controlled process! It is akin to a software app with Hardware. Software controls the laser and wire feed output precisely for different metals and desired weld thicknesses.

# Disadvantage:
**Physics Involved:**
The problem is Heat delivery and conduction. we take for granted that a electric Arc is a very efficient way to deliver Heat to a local region of metal and can reach deep into the metal causing look like welding. Not only is the arc emitting & radiating heat infrared radiation in ultraviolet but the fill metal itself conducts electricity when it's in the path of the ark, or with stick the tip, and experiences Joule (electrical resistance) heating causing it raise in temperature until it's entirely liquid and then deposit that droplet on the substrate being welded. the weld substrate itself is also conducting electricity and its heat is rising too. The laser process is a surface heating effect it cannot penetrate to deeper layers of the metal without thermal conduction through solid metal, which takes longer and makes a temperature gradient, instead of electron flow and joule heating which is more uniform. This reduces the effective weld thickness.

# Use Case:
Handheld laser welding is great for sheet metal and will not Warp as badly! But ineffective for structural steel like I-Beams or thick tubing or plate steel.

Laser is better than MIG welding for single pass on thin metal.

But TIG welding is still competitive with Laser due to Arc control, and deep heat penetration.

As an example, from an engineering standpoint: laser is not something you would use to repair the frame of a vehicle, or weld a tow hitch. But it is perfectly suited for body work and repairing Rust by fabricating body panels.
